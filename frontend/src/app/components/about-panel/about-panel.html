The mission

SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer) is a NASA space telescope launched in March 2025. During its nominal 25-month mission it maps the entire sky several times in near-infrared light, and measures a low-resolution spectrum for every point of it.

What this tool does

Challenger5 finds every SPHEREx image that covers the position you search for, cuts out the area around it and puts the images in order of observation time. Blinking through the observing seasons makes moving objects (nearby stars, brown dwarfs) and brightness changes (variable stars) stand out.

Each image is a narrow slice of infrared colour, so the wavelength slider lets you scan the same patch of sky from 0.75 to 5 µm.

The data

The tool shows SPHEREx Level 2 spectral images: calibrated single exposures in surface-brightness units (MJy/sr), with per-pixel quality flags. They are public and served by the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA). The tool reads the current releases, Data Release 1 and Quick Release 3.

Good to know

  • Images are shown as measured: the glow of dust in the solar system (zodiacal light) is not subtracted.
  • Wavelength changes across every SPHEREx exposure, so the value shown is the one at your target.
  • Stripes, satellite trails and saturated cores of bright stars can appear; “mask bad pixels” in Controls marks the pixels the pipeline flagged.
